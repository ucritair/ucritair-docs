Dashboard: air quality

The Dashboard shows what CAT's sensors measure right now and turns it into a single letter grade.

Opening the Dashboard#

On the home screen, highlight the first tile (clouds and sun) and press A, or tap it. The Dashboard has two pages. Switch between them with ◀ and ▶ (they wrap around). Press B to go home. It always opens on the summary.

LIVE SUMMARY#

A large letter grade for the air overall, from A (best) through A-, B+, B, B-, C+, C, C-, D+, D to F. Its colour follows the grade: green, teal, orange, then red.

To the right of the grade, one box appears for each pollutant group that scores B+ or lower: CO2, fine particles, and gases (VOC or NOx). The number of boxes tells you how many groups need attention; the statistics page tells you which.

LIVE STATISTICS#

One row per sensor: the name, the current value, a colour bar and that sensor's own grade. The marker on the bar sits further right the better the reading, so a marker at the green end is good. Values refresh every 5 seconds.

How the grade is calculated#

Each reading gets a score from 0 (worst) to 1 (best) by sliding between the breakpoints below. The overall grade is the plain average of six scores: the four sensors above plus temperature and humidity.

The scale is strict on purpose. A single sensor drops to F at roughly these readings: CO2 above about 950 ppm, PM2.5 above about 10 µg/m³, a VOC index above about 140, or a NOx index above about 75.

Temperature and humidityCAT has a temperature and humidity sensor, but its readings are not accurate enough yet, so they are hidden. Both count in the grade as a fixed, comfortable 24 °C and 50% humidity. That nudges the overall grade up a little compared with the four sensors alone.

The e-paper air badge#

The separate black-and-white e-paper screen updates every 30 seconds, whatever the main screen is doing. It shows:

  • a unicorn and a cloud whose moods follow the air (happy above 75%, sad below 50%, a smoke cloud below 25%);
  • CO2 in ppm and PM2.5 in µg/m³ in large digits;
  • the NOx and VOC indices;
  • % rebreathed: roughly how much of the air you breathe has already been breathed, estimated from CO2;
  • CritAQI: a percentage driven by the worst pollutant, with a penalty for each other pollutant that is also bad;
  • the time of the reading, and --- for any sensor that failed.

CritAQI is calculated differently from the Dashboard grade (worst pollutant rather than average), so the two can disagree. Faint ghosting between updates is normal; a full refresh clears it about every 25 minutes.
